PPF vs ELSS: Which Tax-Saving Investment Is Better in 2026?

Published 21 August 2026 · 7 min read
Quick answer: PPF suits you if you want guaranteed, government-backed, tax-free returns and can commit money for 15 years. ELSS suits you if you're comfortable with equity market risk in exchange for potentially higher returns and a much shorter 3-year lock-in. Most disciplined investors use both — PPF as the safe anchor, ELSS for growth — since the combined Section 80C limit of ₹1.5 lakh/year applies across whichever instruments you choose.

What is PPF, in one paragraph?

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a government-backed savings scheme currently paying 7.1% per annum, compounded annually. This rate has stayed unchanged since April 2020, one of the longest stable runs in the scheme's history, and is reviewed quarterly by the Ministry of Finance. PPF has a 15-year lock-in (extendable in 5-year blocks after that), and you can invest between ₹500 and ₹1,50,000 per financial year.

What is ELSS, in one paragraph?

An Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) is a mutual fund category that invests primarily in equities and carries a 3-year lock-in — the shortest among all Section 80C options. Unlike PPF, returns aren't fixed or guaranteed; they depend entirely on market performance. Read our full ELSS explainer for how the tax deduction mechanics work.

Why does PPF's 15-year lock-in matter more than it sounds?

Fifteen years is a long time to have money completely inaccessible, with only limited partial withdrawals allowed from the 7th year onward. If there's any real chance you'll need this money within a decade — a house down payment, a child's education, a career change — PPF's illiquidity can work against you regardless of how attractive the guaranteed rate looks on paper.

Why does ELSS's 3-year lock-in matter more than it sounds?

Three years is short by mutual fund standards, but it's not short enough to guarantee you'll exit at a good time. Equity markets can be down at the exact moment your 3-year lock-in ends, forcing a choice between selling at a loss or holding longer without the tax benefit changing. The shorter lock-in is a liquidity advantage, not a guarantee of good timing.

A detail people often miss: SIP investments in ELSS have a lock-in per instalment, not from your first investment date. If you started an ELSS SIP in January 2024, your January 2024 instalment unlocks in January 2027 — but your instalment from June 2024 unlocks in June 2027, and so on.

Can you actually invest in both?

Yes, and this is what most financial planners actually recommend rather than picking one exclusively. Since the Section 80C deduction is capped at ₹1.5 lakh per year combined across PPF, ELSS, EPF, life insurance premiums, and other 80C instruments, the real decision isn't "PPF or ELSS" so much as "what split between them." A common approach: PPF for the portion of your tax-saving allocation you want completely safe, ELSS for the portion you're comfortable exposing to equity growth.

What does this look like with real numbers?

If you invest ₹1,50,000/year for 15 years:

  • All in PPF at 7.1%: matures to approximately ₹40.7 lakh, guaranteed and fully tax-free
  • All in ELSS at an illustrative 12% (not guaranteed): could reach roughly ₹62.9 lakh over the same period, but with real risk of falling short if markets underperform this assumption

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, PPF or ELSS?

Neither is universally better. PPF suits investors who want guaranteed, tax-free returns and can lock money away for 15 years. ELSS suits investors comfortable with market risk who want potentially higher returns and a much shorter 3-year lock-in.

What is the current PPF interest rate in 2026?

7.1% per annum, compounded annually, unchanged since April 2020 and set quarterly by the Ministry of Finance. Confirm the current rate directly at the National Savings Institute.

Can I invest in both PPF and ELSS?

Yes. Many investors split their Section 80C allocation between both. The combined deduction limit stays capped at ₹1.5 lakh/year across all instruments, not per instrument.

What is the lock-in period for PPF vs ELSS?

PPF: 15 years, extendable in 5-year blocks. ELSS: 3 years, the shortest among all Section 80C options.
