FAQ

Common investing & loan questions, answered

The questions people search every day, answered in plain language.

What is a SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)?

A SIP is a way of investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals (usually monthly) instead of investing a lump sum all at once. It builds a savings habit and averages your purchase cost over time. Try our SIP calculator to see how it grows.

What is the minimum amount to start a SIP?

Many mutual funds in India allow SIPs starting from as low as ₹100–₹500 per month, though ₹500–₹1,000 is more common. The minimum varies by fund house and scheme.

What is Nifty 50?

Nifty 50 is the benchmark stock market index of the National Stock Exchange (NSE), tracking 50 of India's largest publicly listed companies. Read our full Sensex vs Nifty explainer.

What is Sensex?

Sensex is the benchmark index of the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE), tracking 30 large, established Indian companies across major sectors.

What is an IPO?

An IPO (Initial Public Offering) is when a private company sells shares to the public for the first time and gets listed on a stock exchange. See our step-by-step guide to applying for an IPO.

How do I open a demat account?

You open a demat account through a SEBI-registered broker (like Zerodha, Upstox, or Groww) by completing an online KYC process with your PAN, Aadhaar, a bank account, and a signature/photo. Most brokers approve accounts within 1–2 business days.

How is EMI calculated on a loan?

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is calculated using the loan amount, interest rate, and tenure, based on a standard reducing-balance formula. Use our loan EMI calculators to see your exact EMI for any loan amount and rate.

What is ELSS and how does it save tax?

ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme) is a mutual fund category eligible for a tax deduction under Section 80C, with a 3-year lock-in. Read our full ELSS tax-saving guide.

What is a Fixed Deposit (FD) and how is interest calculated?

A Fixed Deposit is a lump sum you deposit with a bank for a fixed tenure at a fixed interest rate, usually compounded quarterly in India. Use our FD calculator to see your maturity value.

Is investing in mutual funds safe?

Mutual funds are regulated by SEBI and carry professional management, but they are not risk-free — equity mutual funds are subject to market risk, and returns are never guaranteed. Debt funds carry lower but not zero risk. Diversification helps manage risk but doesn't eliminate it.

What is the difference between SIP and lumpsum investing?

A SIP spreads your investment across regular monthly instalments, averaging your purchase cost. A lumpsum invests everything at once. Read our full SIP vs lumpsum comparison.

How much tax do I pay on stock market gains in India?

As of the framework effective from 23 July 2024 (continuing into FY 2026-27), long-term capital gains (holding over 1 year) above ₹1,00,000 in a financial year are taxed at 12.5%, while short-term gains (under 1 year) are taxed at 20%. Always confirm current rates on the Income Tax Department website, since rules can change with each Union Budget.

What is the repo rate and how does it affect me?

The repo rate is the interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) lends to commercial banks. When it changes, floating-rate home loan EMIs and FD/savings interest rates typically move in the same direction, with some lag.

What is the difference between a Gold ETF and physical gold?

A Gold ETF trades on the stock exchange like a share and is backed by physical gold held by the fund, making it easy to buy/sell via a demat account. Physical gold involves storage risk and making charges. See our full gold investment comparison.

What documents are needed for a personal loan?

Typically: PAN card, Aadhaar or another address proof, recent salary slips or income tax returns, and bank statements from the last 3–6 months. Exact requirements vary by lender.

What is a money horoscope?

A money horoscope is a light, sign-based daily reading about financial mood or energy, similar to a newspaper horoscope column. It's for entertainment and reflection only, not a real predictor of market or investment outcomes. Try ours on the Money Horoscope section.

Is a financial horoscope accurate for predicting the stock market?

No — astrology has no scientific or financial predictive basis for stock market movements. A money horoscope is a fun, reflective daily read, not a trading or investment signal. Real investment decisions should be based on your goals, research, and if needed, a SEBI-registered advisor.
